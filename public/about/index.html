About

Find. Trust. Understand.

Open data is only useful if people can actually find it, judge it and understand it.

A dataset can be legally open and still be hard to discover, difficult to assess or too technical for many people to use with confidence.

Belgium Data Desk is an independent project trying to contribute to that broader challenge.

The direction is simple: help make Belgian open data easier to find, trust and understand.

Find

Belgian public data is spread across federal, regional, local and specialist portals. That fragmentation makes sense organisationally, but it makes discovery harder for everyone else.

We are working on bringing more of those sources together in one searchable catalogue and making them easier to browse.

We are also exposing the catalogue through MCP so AI assistants can help people discover relevant datasets without first knowing which portal to search.

And discovery should not depend on somebody actively visiting a data catalogue. Through Belgian Open Data on social media, we are also trying to surface interesting datasets, examples and findings to a wider audience.

These are different ways of contributing to the same goal: making public data easier to find.

Trust

Open data should not be treated as trustworthy simply because it has been published.

People need context.

Who published it? When was it updated? What licence applies? Is there a contact? Does the download still work? Is the file actually what the catalogue says it is?

We are working on making more of that evidence visible and automating checks where that is useful and realistic.

Those checks do not prove that a dataset is correct. They give people more information to make their own judgement and make problems easier to notice and report.

Understand

To understand data, you need to see it.

That means being able to look beyond a title and description and inspect what is actually inside: the columns, values, structure, gaps and relationships.

We are experimenting with previews, profiling and browser-based querying with DuckDB, and we want to explore lower-friction ways to compare and combine datasets.

Quizzes and explainers are another part of that effort. They can help people become more comfortable reading public data critically and recognising its limitations.

What we are working towards

Belgium Data Desk is not intended to become the final destination for Belgian open data, and it does not replace the organisations that publish it.

The publishers remain the source.

We want to contribute useful tools, checks, experiments and ways of discovering data that can help move the wider open-data ecosystem in the right direction.

If some of those ideas make public data a little easier to find, a little easier to assess and a little easier to understand, then the project is doing something useful.

Publishing data is an important part of openness. Making it genuinely accessible and usable is a much broader effort, and this project is one contribution to that.
